R0 and the effective R

R0 is how many people one case infects when nobody is immune. As people recover or are vaccinated, each case finds fewer people to infect, so the effective R falls. The outbreak shrinks once it drops below 1.

Why flattening matters

Distancing does not always change how many people get infected in the end, but it spreads them out. A lower, later peak can mean everyone who needs a hospital bed gets one.

What the model leaves out

Real populations are not perfectly mixed: age, households, travel and changing behaviour all matter, and immunity can fade. SEIR is the starting point epidemiologists build on, not a forecast.

Parameters and presets are illustrative, chosen to show the model's behaviour, not to describe any real disease precisely. The model is the standard SEIR compartmental model, solved in small daily steps. Educational only; not medical or public-health advice.
