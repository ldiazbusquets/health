See how medical AI gets things right, and wrong.

Each one is a working model you can push around. Change a number and watch the consequences, the way a clinician, a regulator or a data scientist would.

The SEIR model epidemiologists use. Set how contagious a disease is, then try vaccination and distancing and watch the curve and the hospital load change.

Algorithm Bias

A risk model trained mostly on one group quietly fails another. Change the training data and see who the model misses.

Accuracy is not one number. It depends on who you test, how common the disease is, and who the model learned from.

About

Why a finance student builds health tools.

The same questions run through finance and medicine: how much to trust a model, what an error costs, and who carries that cost. This site works through them for healthcare as part of the Applied AI minor at Bryant University.

It is a sister site to the LDB Lab, which covers how machine learning works and how it is used in finance. Everything here runs in your browser; nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

The heart at the top of the page is real anatomy, drawn as dots: the heart walls, coronary vessels, aorta and pulmonary vessels from BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, used under CC BY 4.0 and adapted (parts extracted, vessels trimmed, geometry merged).

Educational only. These tools use made-up or illustrative numbers to explain ideas. They are not medical advice and say nothing about any real person's health.

Thanks for stopping by. Stay curious, stay well.
